Privacy policy

Gridiron Duel, by playbyear. Last updated November 25, 2026.

Gridiron Duel is a football play-calling game you play against the computer, with a friend on one device, or with a friend online. It has no accounts, no ads and no tracking, and it never asks for your name or any other personal information. This page explains the little information the game handles and where it goes.

What stays on your device

The game in progress, your sound setting, and a random code the game makes up for your device are kept on your phone or computer: in the browser's storage on the website, and in the app's own storage in the iPhone app. The app also remembers the code of an online game you're in, so it can rejoin it when you open the app again. A game against the computer or on one device is played entirely on your device, apart from the anonymous play counts described below. Deleting the app, or clearing the website's data in your browser, deletes what's stored.

Online games, if you play them

Playing a friend online is optional. When you create or join an online game, the game is stored in an online database run with Google's Firebase service, so both players' devices can follow it. An online game holds:

  • its four-letter game code,
  • the team names and colors the two coaches chose, the game settings, and every play call and result,
  • the time of the last move, and
  • each coach's random device code, so the game knows which team is yours when you come back to it.

There are no messages or chat. The only words a player types are team names, so don't use your real name as one if you'd rather not. Nobody can list or browse the games; a game can only be found with its code, which you share with your friend yourself. A game that nobody has touched for a week is deleted.

Anonymous play counts

To see how much the game is played, it counts games started and finished each day in the same database. Each count records only the day, where the game was played (the iPhone app, the website, or itch.io), and how (online, against the computer at each difficulty, or on one device), plus the random device codes seen that day, for a rough count of players. The counts include no names, no team names and nothing about what happened in a game, and they aren't linked to you or used for advertising.

Google's part

When the game connects to the database, Google receives your device's internet address as part of the connection, as any website does. Google handles it under its own terms as a service provider; the game doesn't keep or use it. You can read Firebase's privacy information.

Children

The game isn't aimed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect personal information from them. It never asks for any.

Changes

If the game ever handles information differently, this page will be updated first, with a new date at the top.

Contact

Questions, problems, or anything about your privacy: use the contact form, and say that it's about Gridiron Duel. You don't need an account, and only the game's developer sees what you send. Add your email address only if you'd like a reply; it's used just to answer you. Messages are kept in Google Forms, under Google's terms.
